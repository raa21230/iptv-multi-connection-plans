## What Does “Multi Connection” Actually Mean?

Think about a normal evening at home.

One person is watching sports on the living-room TV. Someone else turns on a movie in the bedroom. Another family member starts a different show on a tablet.

That is three active streams.

A **1-connection subscription** is built for one screen streaming at a time. A **3-connection subscription** is designed for three simultaneous streams. TiviLive says its multi-connection plans support up to five concurrent connections, and its FAQ defines a connection as the number of devices that can stream at the same time. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

That distinction matters because installing an IPTV app on five devices is not necessarily the same thing as watching on five devices simultaneously. Multi-connection plans are about active streams, not simply how many devices have the app installed. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

## Which Connection Level Makes Sense?

For a single viewer, one connection is the obvious starting point. There is little reason to pay for extra simultaneous streams if one screen is all you normally use.

Two connections make more sense for a couple or a home where the TV and a second screen are commonly active together.

Three connections is where things start getting interesting for a family. One person can watch live TV in the living room while another uses a bedroom TV and someone else watches on a tablet.

Four and five connections are aimed at busier households where different people regularly watch different content at the same time.
